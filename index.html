01

Chemisorption is widely used to capture CO₂ from industrial gas streams, but predicting its behaviour over time remains challenging. In this work, Physics-informed Neural Ordinary Differential Equation (Neural ODE) models were developed to predict CO₂ absorption and chemical consumption in a packed-bed system. Additionally, physics guided-enhancements were incorporated into the models through soft constraints in the training loss function and additional collocation points, enabling the models to learn from both experimental data and governing physical behaviour. Rigorous data ablation and statistical significance tests were conducted as well.

Fluidized beds are widely used across industries, yet their behaviour with non-Newtonian fluids remains less understood than that of conventional Newtonian systems. Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD), specifically ANSYS Fluent was employed, to model the complex interactions between solid particles and non-Newtonian fluids. The study investigates fluidization behaviour, including bed expansion, voidage and heat transfer, with a particular focus on understanding how flow pulsation influences heat transfer performance.

In collaboration withNational University of Singapore

This study investigates the dynamics of two interlocked catenane rings under Brownian motion and elongational flow. Their relaxation behaviour and response to flow are characterised using relaxation time and the Weissenberg number, with comparisons made against linear polymer chains and single-ring systems. The work explores how polymer topology and interlocking influence molecular motion and deformation under different flow conditions.

Characterising the composition of polymer waste is essential for improving plastic recycling and enabling more effective material recovery. This work combines hyperspectral imaging (HSI) with deep learning to simultaneously identify and quantify multiple additives in polypropylene-based polymer blends. The approach enables the detection of components such as polyethylene, talc and flame retardants from their spectral signatures, while predicting their concentrations and presence within a single framework.

This work explores the conversion of food waste into a valuable feedstock for lactic acid production. Moisture and pathogens are first removed using a treatment technology developed by Cora Environment, followed by the extraction of glucose from the processed material. The glucose-rich substrate is then fermented using Lactobacillus helveticus to produce lactic acid, demonstrating a potential pathway for transforming food waste into higher-value bioproducts.

01

Chemometric Intern · A*STAR (SIMTech)

Built and curated near-infrared (NIR), Fourier-transform infrared (FTIR) and melt flow index databases covering the different types of plastic waste, recording for every sample both its polymer identity and the quantity of each common additive it carried. Holding all three measurements on one consistent footing is what gave model training a well-characterised dataset.

Researched and benchmarked a range of dimensionality reduction tools and machine learning models against that dataset, comparing how each behaved where the signatures of chemically similar polymers overlap — the region that decides whether a sorting call is right or wrong — then tuning the pipeline to optimise classification accuracy.

Key Achievements: Supported the development of predictive models achieving R² values of 0.925–0.979 with RMSE values of 0.677–1.785 wt% across the additive concentrations covered by the database — accurate enough for the spectra to stand in for a separate composition measurement.
